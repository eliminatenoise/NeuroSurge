# A wrist lift and a balance response

Matthew D. Luciw, Ewa Jarocka and Benoni B. Edin recorded people gripping and lifting an object with their right hand after a light cue. Surface electrodes recorded electrical activity over five right arm and hand muscles at 4,000 Hz. A tracker sampled wrist position at 500 Hz. The forecast starts 400 ms after the light. Positions are in the tracker’s X, Y and Z frame, converted from centimetres to millimetres. The released muscle amplitudes retain their source units because their conversion to volts has not been established. The Figshare records carry a CC0 notice and the descriptor carries CC BY 4.0. [WAY-EEG-GAL collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.988376.v2) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2014.47) · [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Steven M. Peterson and Daniel P. Ferris recorded different people standing or walking as a headset scene rotated. Four pairs of left and right leg muscles supply amplitudes in microvolts. An optical marker tracks the sacrum at the back of the pelvis. In the distributed study, leg signals and marker positions share a 256 Hz sampling grid. The forecast starts 125 to just under 129 ms after rotation begins. The three marker axes follow the laboratory’s side-to-side, vertical and front-to-back coordinates. The anatomical signs remain unresolved. [Recording, version 1.0.3](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds003739.v1.0.3) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2021.107635) · [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)

Each model receives 625 ms of earlier muscle amplitude and marker movement, in twenty bins, and a mask identifying which named channels supplied a signal. It returns the root-mean-square amplitude of each available channel over the next 125 ms and the marker’s full path at 32 times in that interval, measured from the last observed position. The two experiments have distinct markers, muscles, frames, participants and units. There were no paired wrist-and-pelvis recordings across the two studies.

The model labelled **Both studies** learned a shared temporal representation from the two sources. **This task only** uses the same architecture trained only on the selected study, with the same number of training batches from that study. The selectors run both fitted models on two mathematically generated examples. Changing a scale edits all earlier muscle amplitudes or marker displacements for that example. The wrist example leaves the flexor digitorum channel unavailable. Its missing signal yields no forecast for that muscle. Neither example has a measured future against which a prediction can be checked.

## How far off were the forecasts?

Three grasp-and-lift participants and six visual balance participants were outside fitting. Their recordings had been examined for earlier outcomes. Only windows with enough reliable muscle channels and marker support throughout the earlier and future interval qualified. That last condition means the score describes good recordings selected with knowledge of future signal quality. In the wrist assessment, none of the three participants had reliable data on all five channels.

| 882 wrist lifts | Muscle amplitude (source units) | 0.00785 | 0.00778 | 0.00764 |
| Same lifts | Wrist path (mm) | 4.87 | 5.10 | 4.39 |
| 1,759 visual rotations | Muscle amplitude (µV) | 10.28 | 10.27 | 10.27 |
| Same rotations | Sacrum path (mm) | 2.17 | 1.96 | 1.98 |

Each person counts equally. Muscle error is the mean absolute difference from the measured RMS amplitude across available channels. Path error is the mean of the three-dimensional distances from the 32 measured future positions. Both models shown in the game received 192 updates from the selected task. The two-study fit received 192 more from the other study. The final column gives those extra 192 updates to a separate single-task fit. Only the original two fits power the interactive graphs.

Against the single-task models shown in the game, the two-study fit lowered wrist path error by 0.230 mm (95% paired interval 0.174 to 0.294 mm lower) and increased sacrum path error by 0.207 mm (0.167 to 0.242 mm higher). Its changes in muscle amplitude error were +0.000062 source units (−0.000040 to +0.000114) and +0.0074 µV (−0.0056 to +0.0173). These intervals resample whole people 5,000 times. With three wrist participants the range of possible resamples is small. The two-study model failed the four-outcome improvement test against both single-task comparisons. The measurements concern these tasks and recording conditions.
