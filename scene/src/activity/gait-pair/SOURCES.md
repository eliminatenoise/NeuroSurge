# Right heel and plate-force forecast

Céline Schreiber and Florent Moissenet’s gait study recorded barefoot adults under several speed instructions. Their version 8 deposit carries Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. This model uses the slow metronome condition (C2, target 0.4–0.8 m/s), right posterior heel and second-metatarsal-head skin markers, eight right-leg surface EMGs and the force plate assigned to the right foot. The previous 24 marker frames at 100 Hz end at the cutoff. Each of the four EMG bins summarizes 90 samples at 1,500 Hz. The model also receives the time since the annotated right foot strike. Original filtering and contact selection used retrospective information. [Versioned data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.7734767.v8) · [Experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0124-4) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

NeuroTranslate fitted a shared temporal transformer to predict two measurements 100 ms later. One is the change in heel position in each laboratory axis relative to the last input, in millimetres. Positive Z points up. The other is the mean upward force over 15 samples centred at the target, in newtons. Absolute XY directions depend on the laboratory coordinate frame. The game can run the fitted parameters from local files. The example positions and EMG amplitudes are invented. Moving the slider adds the same gradual change to both markers' lab X histories and preserves each final position. There is no measured future for these examples. The avatar takes movement commands separately, and camera takes contain projected limb angles.

Development used three participant-separated outer folds and nested training-side selection. Each participant contributed equally, then each eligible repeat, then each window. Across 203 overlapping examples from 61 repeats and 25 people the joint model's mean Euclidean heel error was 1.365 mm (participant bootstrap 95% interval 1.183–1.591 mm), and its mean absolute force error was 100.03 N (80.02–121.06 N). The marker-and-time-only transformer scored 1.251 mm and 89.87 N. Separate specialists scored 1.364 mm and 99.45 N. Neither a joint-over-specialist advantage nor an incremental EMG advantage met the study criterion. The resampling intervals describe these participants and this analysis. The two decimal places in the example table help compare inputs. They do not establish that level of physical accuracy.
