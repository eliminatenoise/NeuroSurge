# Walking “as usual” or “fast but not running”

Céline Schreiber and Florent Moissenet recorded adults walking barefoot across a 10 m walkway at the Rehazenter in Luxembourg. Two self-selected conditions, C4 and C5, carry distinct instructions in the published protocol. Those words describe the whole trial. The deposited data contain optical marker recordings and annotated foot strikes. They contain no recording or timestamp for an individual spoken instruction. [Study and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0124-4) · [Walking data, version 8](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.7734767.v8) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

NeuroTranslate fits two temporal transformers to predict travel 100 ms after a cutoff. One sees the condition code. The other learns without it. Both see the same 21 frames of pelvis-centre and right-heel marker positions at 100 Hz and the elapsed time from right foot strike. The pelvis centre is the mean of four surface markers. Forward follows its net horizontal travel during the observed 200 ms. Left is the perpendicular horizontal axis and positive Z points up. Each output is a landmark displacement from its own final observed position, in millimetres. This forecast has no force, muscle voltage or neural output.

The game bundles fitted parameters, inference code and an invented history. Each time you change the instruction, both forecasts run locally. The input history is identical for the two choices. There is no measured future for the invented walk. Both condition labels map to categories. The study does not test understanding of arbitrary speech. The C camera and the game's movement controller are separate features.

The fits used 30 development participants. Ten different participants, previously used in other walking assessments, contributed 60 walks and 216 overlapping forecast windows. Each participant counted equally. The 100 ms mean endpoint errors with the correct instruction were 5.02 mm (pelvis) and 5.74 mm (right heel). Without a cue they were 5.12 and 5.68 mm. Giving the opposite instruction increased the errors to 5.49 and 6.26 mm. The paired 95% bootstrap intervals for correct minus no-cue error were −0.23 to +0.02 mm for the pelvis and −0.13 to +0.26 mm for the heel. Neither landmark met the study's requirement for added predictive value over both controls.
