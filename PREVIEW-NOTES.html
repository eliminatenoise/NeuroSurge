NeuroSurge preview notes

Both editions use the same 3D game. Aim at part of the motor strip to move the body and see the same movement on the mirror body.

Camera, movement and tutorial

The camera responds to your own look. Limb selection, joint movement and brain activity no longer turn the view or push the lens closer. Press E to switch between the eye view and a fixed-distance outside orbit. Moving a limb can still take it out of the picture, depending on where you are looking.

In the eye view, a small look up or down moves the selected arm at the shoulder or leg at the hip. The aiming cue stays near the upper quarter of the screen. Press Escape and open View tutorial for visual steps. After choosing a limb, practise the shoulder or hip motion with the on-screen marker. Drag on an empty part of the picture to look. Resume keeps the pointer free. Choose Capture mouse in the controls if you want to turn the view with each mouse movement. The tutorial marks brain areas and selected body parts in the game.

Faint strokes run across the brain and motor strip in the normal view. You can turn them off in Escape settings. M opens the detailed electrical map. Both effects depict activity made by the game.

Command box and camera

To follow a saved file in the standalone game, press C to open the recorder. From the local model launcher, choose Record a limb take. Set Source to Recorded video, choose the limbs under Follow, select a local file and choose Play video. Keep up to eight seconds of tracked movement with Save video take, then press F and enter “replay my take”. The file stays on this device.

Choose Timed pictures to use two or more local images. Assign a time to each, choose Track pictures, then Save picture take. Press F and enter “replay my take” to animate the body between the tracked poses. The images stay on this device.

Press F to enter a supported movement. In the standalone game, press C to open the recorder. Choose the limbs under Follow before selecting Start camera. Use the arrow keys to change the choice while Follow has focus. Once video starts, a green line and Following appear for each limb the recorder can use. Show a bend means the recorder can see the knee and needs a clear starting bend. Bring into view means it could not use that limb in the picture. The game can replay a saved take after the camera stops.

With the local model launcher, press C to open the Movement mirror. Use Body follows to pick the limbs the camera or recording will move. Choose Forecast only to keep the game’s manual body controls. Use Point to forecast separately. The forecast can use a point on a different limb. Pause or seek a clip to release the tracked limbs. A clip or still sequence releases them when it finishes.

The scene, virtual joint targets and the optional model forecasts serve different purposes. The game can use estimated image points to move its body. The local model launcher can use those points to forecast movement. The electrical activity remains simulated.

Current limits

Verification used desktop Linux Chromium. Testers used the normal browser routes, a mouse and keyboard, and the actual rendered camera. Native Windows and macOS launcher use and play with physical touch input were outside that check.

The brain model and the body approximate real anatomy. The coloured band follows a fold called the precentral gyrus. Its exposed surface includes a movement-planning area (premotor cortex, BA6). Much of the primary motor cortex (BA4) is in the front wall of the groove behind it. The coloured blocks are representative game controls. The game measures no brain signal, muscle force or muscle length. It cannot tell how a specific person’s nervous system works.
