NeuroSurge Lite Simple guide

Move a body part

Start with the motor strip. It has 19 areas you can choose from. Each one moves a part of your virtual body. Choose Elbow under Left body, for example, and you will see the left arm bend at the elbow in both views. Choose Straighten elbow to extend it again. You can leave the elbow bent and move a knee without straightening the arm.

Use the movement buttons below the strip to choose the pair of moves for the current area. On a keyboard, press 1 for the first move and 2 for the second. If you are focused on an area, use the arrow keys to choose another one. Clicking or tapping a visible part of either body also selects and moves that part. Choose Neutral pose to return all the parts to where they started.

Read the motor strip

A hemisphere is one half of the brain. In this game the right half controls parts on the left side of your body, and the left half controls parts on the right. The trunk, head and jaw have shared buttons that sit below the two sides. The order roughly follows a real movement map from foot toward hand and face. The game uses distinct buttons so you can choose one move at a time. In a real brain the areas overlap, and other nearby areas help coordinate movements.

Turn the bodies

Your body is the virtual body you move. Mirror body shows the same pose reflected at the same size. Try bending one knee and compare the figures. Each screen shows the facing side of its figure, so the reflection can put a limb on the other side of the picture. The body part you control keeps its name. The highlight identifies the selected part on both figures.

Drag on either figure to turn the two views together. You can also select Turn left or Turn right. Restore view brings both views back to their starting angle.

Body shape and motion

The game uses linked three-dimensional body parts. A movement turns one or more parts at a joint. Turning a palm, for example, turns the forearm around its length independently of the upper arm. The approximate lengths come from a published adult anatomy model. The game uses a simplified surface around those parts to keep them recognizable from different angles.

The pictures are drawn by the browser as the body moves or the view changes. Drawing stops once everything is still. If you use your system’s reduced-motion setting, the body goes straight to the new pose. The movements and highlights are simulated. To calculate the forces in muscles or model physical contact, a separate physics model, such as MyoSuite, is needed.

Open a downloaded game

Download the ZIP, extract it and keep the files together. You can open index.html in your browser. You can also use the launcher for your computer, Linux.sh, macOS.command or Windows.cmd. Use Original game to go back to the existing NeuroSurge release.
