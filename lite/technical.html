NeuroSurge Lite Technical guide

Motor strip and controls

Select one of 19 motor areas to apply its first movement. The two hemisphere columns map to opposite sides of the body. Trunk, head and jaw use shared controls. The order is a simplified movement map from foot toward hand and face. The divisions are game controls. Primary motor cortex has overlapping representations and areas involved in coordinating movement.

Use the two movement buttons or keys 1 and 2 to apply either movement for the selected area. With a motor area focused, the arrow keys change the selection. Select or tap a visible body part to use its first movement. Joint positions persist while other parts move. Neutral pose brings all joints back to their starting positions.

Body and mirror

Both views consume the same joint values. Linked segments produce the movement by rotating at their joints. Forearm rotation is defined independently of shoulder rotation. Approximate segment lengths come from the BodyParts3D atlas and its joint landmarks. The body surfaces are shaded, tapered 3D shapes. The selected control highlights the corresponding part in both figures.

The mirror reflects body coordinates through a vertical plane at the same scale. Each image uses the perspective of its facing side. Drag either figure or use Turn left and Turn right to rotate the view. Restore view returns both figures to their starting angle.

Runtime and files

The game renders with browser Canvas 2D and a three-dimensional geometric model. It redraws during changes of pose, view or viewport size and stops drawing at rest. With reduced motion enabled, the joint reaches its new pose without interpolation. Positions and highlights are simulated. Muscle forces and physical contact require a dynamics model such as MyoSuite.

Extract the ZIP and keep its files together. Open index.html, Linux.sh, macOS.command or Windows.cmd. Original game opens the existing NeuroSurge release.
