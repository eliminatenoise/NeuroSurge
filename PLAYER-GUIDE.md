# Play NeuroSurge

Open https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/. The page runs locally in your browser after downloading its files. See [preview notes](PREVIEW-NOTES.html) for what is new, the current limitations and credits. For offline local hosting, run `node serve-local.mjs` and open the printed address.

Click the scene once so the mouse looks around. That first click does nothing else. The start page shows a picture of the keys. Esc brings it back with the short instructions, and **Show the full instructions** on that page has the rest.

The glass brain is in the upper part of the view and the mirror body is below it. The coloured band on the brain is the motor strip, a simplified map of the body: each half of the brain mainly moves the opposite side of the body. A “Try this” line near the top gives you one small thing to try at a time.

- **Look** with the mouse or trackpad. Your head and neck turn, and past their reach your chest turns and your trunk bends. Your left hand keeps its place in the view.
- **Rest your look** on a coloured motor area for about a third of a second, or **click** it. That body part jerks as far as it can go and stays there. The area and the muscles it drives flash once, like a neon tube being switched on, and then glow together on the brain and on both bodies; a line in the same colour joins the area to its muscles. A quick glance only lights the area. Your look reaches an area even when your own body or the mirror body is in front of it; the “Looking at” card says what is in front.
- **Read what you are looking at.** The “Looking at” card at the bottom left names the motor area and then its movement, for example “Left knee · Bend left knee”. The information card on the right names the part of the strip and the half of the brain the area is drawn on, and lists the area's movements with numbers. The card takes a new area when your look has rested on it for about a third of a second, so areas you only cross on the way do not change it.
- **Press a number, 1 to 9,** to do the movement with that number on the card. The list belongs to the area you look at, or the one you looked at last, so it stays while you look down at the body to watch. A movement that would not move the body from where it is now is marked “already there”.
- **← and →** put your look on the next motor area toward that side of the screen, in the order of the body along the strip. Hold a key to keep stepping. A step is an ordinary look: rest there and the part moves. Areas hidden in the current view are passed over, and the keys wait while Space holds something.
- **Bring a part back** by switching on the opposite area, which glows faintly, or with **Reset body** on the Esc page.
- **Legs.** A knee bends all the way, and the hip area swings the thigh down and back as well as up, out and in. A leg that swings down can leave your own view; watch it on the mirror body.
- **Click a muscle** on your own body or on the mirror body to switch on the motor area that moves it. The “Looking at” card names the muscle, bone, area or arrow under the crosshair before you click. Bones do nothing.
- **W/A/S/D** move your left hand: W forward, S back, A left, D right. The shoulder part of the left-arm strip lights (and the elbow part for W and S), with the muscles that make the movement, and the information card names the key, the strip part, the movement and the muscles.
- **Hold Space** on the glass brain, the mirror body or one of your limbs to take hold of it. Your left hand closes while the hold lasts, and its strip part lights. While Space is held, W moves the held thing farther, S closer, A/D sideways, and your look carries it up and down. The mirror body stays on the ground. Let go of Space to leave it there.
- **Tap Space** once to take hold and keep holding after the tap. Tap Space again to let go.
- **Tap Space twice** and all four limbs turn once: both forearms turn the palms up or down, and both upper arms and both hips turn inward or outward. **Tap twice and hold** and they keep turning, slowly. Let go and every limb stays where it is.
- **Hold Shift** as you click, press Space or drag to reach the other thing on the same line of sight: the muscle in front of a motor area, the brain behind the mirror body, or what is behind an arrow.
- **Press and drag** on the brain or the mirror body to turn it. Drag the mirror body up or down to slide it away or back. Drag across one of your limbs to turn it like a dial.
- **Click an arrow** for a small step, or hold it to keep going. The grey arrows turn the brain in place. The green arrows by the mirror body turn it (left and right) or slide it away and back (top and bottom). Press Esc twice for a free pointer if you want to click them while you watch.
- **E** switches to a view from above and behind your right shoulder, and back. The mirror body moves away by the same distance as the camera, so it does not end up close to the camera. Let go of what Space holds first: while Space holds something, E only shows a line that says so.
- **R** reads information. The side cards are on at the start. Press R for More detail and the colour key, R again to hide all of it, and R again to bring the cards back.
- **Esc** pauses and shows the instructions. **Reset body** puts every body part back where it started. **Reset brain view** puts the brain and the mirror body back. The settings there change the look speed, whether up and down are flipped, the picture quality and the lifted outer muscles, and hide or restart the “Try this” guide. Thin outlines between limbs are drawn at every picture quality; the soft shading in the creases between muscles is drawn only at full quality.

Brain and muscle signals are simulated and unitless. A clicked muscle lights a whole motor area, not a spot for that one muscle. A movement staying until its opposite is a rule of this game. Movement limits, physiological mappings and movement mechanics are simplified and still need checking.
