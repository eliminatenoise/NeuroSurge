# Play NeuroSurge

Open https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/. The page runs locally in your browser after downloading its files. See [preview notes](PREVIEW-NOTES.html) for what is new, the current limitations and credits. For offline local hosting, run `node serve-local.mjs` and open the printed address.

There is no start page. While the files load you watch the bones, the muscles and the parts of the brain come together, and for the first five seconds the main controls are shown beside them: W A S D move, Space grabs, E changes the view, R shows information; on the trackpad or mouse, moving looks, a click presses and a drag turns. The game then begins by itself. Click the scene once so the pointer looks around; that first click does nothing else. Esc shows the instructions at any time.

You are slumped back as on a sofa, looking diagonally down your own front. From the bottom of the view up you see your collar bones and both shoulders, your chest and stomach, and your legs running out to your feet. The brain lies beyond and below your feet, large and beating slowly, with the middle of its coloured strip showing between your legs; the crosshair starts there. Your legs hide part of the strip: the crosshair still takes an area behind a leg, and Shift takes the leg. The mirror body stands just above your feet. The coloured band on the brain is the motor strip, a simplified map of the body: each half of the brain mainly moves the opposite side of the body. Think of it as a control panel: each coloured area is a button. One short line at the top gives you one small thing to try at a time.

- **Look** with the mouse or trackpad. Your head and neck turn, past their reach your chest turns and your trunk bends, and past the end of that your eyes turn the rest of the way: you can look all the way round, nearly straight down and up, and over your shoulder at your own back. The muscles that turn your head light as you look. Your left hand keeps its place in the view.
- **Rest your look** on a coloured motor area for about a third of a second, or **click** it. That body part jerks as far as it can go and stays there. The area and the muscles it drives flash once, like a neon tube being switched on, and then glow together on the brain and on both bodies; a line in the same colour joins the area to its muscles. A quick glance only lights the area. Your look reaches an area even when your own body or the mirror body is in front of it; the “Looking at” card says what is in front.
- **Read what you are looking at.** A small tip beside the crosshair names the motor area under it and lists its movements with numbers, for example “Left elbow: 1 Bend, 2 Straighten”. On a body it names the muscle or bone under the crosshair. That tip, the short line at the top and the names on the brain are all the text the game starts with. **R** brings the side cards with the fuller text: the “Looking at” card at the bottom left and the information card on the right, which names the part of the strip, the half of the brain, the signal path and the muscles. R again adds More detail and the colour key, and R again hides the cards.
- **Press a number, 1 to 9,** to do the movement with that number in the tip (or on the card). The list belongs to the area you look at, or the one you looked at last, so the keys still work while you look down at the body to watch. A movement that would not move the body from where it is now is marked “already there”. A movement whose part is held the other way is marked “first press brings it back”: the first press only returns the part to where it started, and the next press does the movement. The card at the bottom left says which of the two a key did.
- **← and →** put your look on the next motor area toward that side of the screen, in the order of the body along the strip. Hold a key to keep stepping. A step is an ordinary look: rest there and the part moves. Areas hidden in the current view are passed over, and the keys wait while Space holds something.
- **Bring a part back** by switching on the opposite area, which glows faintly, by pressing the number of the opposite movement on the card, or with **Reset body** on the Esc page. A part held one way first comes back to where it started; switching on the opposite once more takes it the other way.
- **Head and trunk.** A trunk movement bends the trunk under your head, so your view stays where it is. While you look up at the brain a forward bend is drawn smaller than when you look level or down. A head movement moves your view with it, because you see from your head. The card at the bottom left then says how to bring your head back, and names the number key when the card on the right has one. In this game a head turn is brought back from the neck area on the other half of the brain.
- **Legs.** A knee bends all the way, and the hip area swings the thigh down and back as well as up, out and in. A leg that swings down can leave your own view; watch it on the mirror body.
- **Click a muscle** on your own body or on the mirror body to switch on the motor area that moves it. The “Looking at” card names the muscle, bone, area or arrow under the crosshair before you click. Bones do nothing.
- **W/A/S/D** move your left hand: W forward, S back, A left, D right. The shoulder part of the left-arm strip lights (and the elbow part for W and S), with the muscles that make the movement, and the information card names the key, the strip part, the movement and the muscles.
- **Hold Space** on the brain, the mirror body or one of your limbs to take hold of it. Both hands close while the hold lasts. Then **move the pointer**: the held thing moves left, right, up and down across the view, and the view stays where it is. The mirror body lifts off the ground this way. **W** moves the held thing farther, **S** nearer, **A/D** sideways. The hold keeps its thing wherever the crosshair goes. Let go of Space to leave it there.
- **Tap Space** once to take hold and keep holding after the tap. Tap Space again to let go.
- **The mirror body.** Take it by its head or trunk and you move all of it. Take one of its arms or legs and you move your own matching limb, which the mirror body shows: the limb goes the way the pointer goes on the mirror body.
- The game starts with the underside of your limbs showing: palms up, soles turned outward. **Tap Space twice** and all four limbs switch quickly to the overside (palms down, soles inward); tap twice again and they switch back. The upper arms and thighs only follow by a few degrees. **Tap twice and hold** and the limbs keep turning slowly between the two sides. Let go and every limb stays where it is.
- **Hold Shift** as you click, press Space or drag to reach the other thing on the same line of sight: the muscle in front of a motor area, the brain behind the mirror body, or what is behind an arrow.
- **Press and drag** on the brain or the mirror body to turn it. Drag the mirror body sideways to turn it round, all the way if you like, and up or down to tilt it, until you see it from straight above or below. Drag across one of your limbs to turn it like a dial.
- **Click an arrow** for a small step, or hold it to keep going. The grey arrows turn the brain in place. The green arrows by the mirror body turn it round (left and right) or tilt it (the top one shows it more from above, the bottom one more from below). A turned or tilted mirror body stands a little farther back where it needs the room. Reset brain view on the Esc page stands it upright again, facing you. Press Esc twice for a free pointer if you want to click them while you watch.
- **E** switches to a view from above and behind your right shoulder, and back. The mirror body moves away by the same distance as the camera, so it does not end up close to the camera. Let go of what Space holds first: while Space holds something, E only shows a line that says so.
- **R** shows more information. The game starts with the cards off. Press R for the side cards, R again for More detail and the colour key, and R again to hide them.
- **Esc** pauses and shows the instructions. **Reset body** puts every body part back where it started. **Reset brain view** puts the brain and the mirror body back. The settings there change the look speed, whether up and down are flipped, the picture quality and the lifted outer muscles, and hide or restart the “Try this” guide. Thin outlines between limbs are drawn at every picture quality; the soft shading in the creases between muscles is drawn only at full quality.

Brain and muscle signals are simulated and unitless. A clicked muscle lights a whole motor area, not a spot for that one muscle. A movement staying until its opposite is a rule of this game. Movement limits, physiological mappings and movement mechanics are simplified and still need checking.
