# Play NeuroSurge

Open https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/. The page runs locally in your browser after downloading its files. See [preview notes](PREVIEW-NOTES.html) for what is new, the current limitations and credits. For offline local hosting, run `node serve-local.mjs` and open the printed address.

There is no start page. While the files load, which takes a few seconds, a bar with a percentage shows the progress, and for the first five seconds the main controls are shown: W A S D move, Space grabs, E changes the view, R shows information; on the trackpad or mouse, moving looks, a click presses and a drag turns. The game then begins by itself. Click the scene once so the pointer looks around; that first click does nothing else. Esc shows the instructions at any time.

You are slumped back as on a sofa, looking diagonally down your own front. From the bottom of the view up you see your collar bones and both shoulders, your chest and stomach, and your legs running out to your feet. The brain lies beyond and below your feet, large and beating slowly, with the middle of its coloured strip showing between your legs; the crosshair starts there. Your legs hide part of the strip: the crosshair still takes an area behind a leg, and Shift takes the leg. The mirror body stands just above your feet. The coloured band on the brain is the motor strip, a simplified map of the body: each half of the brain mainly moves the opposite side of the body. Think of it as a control panel: each coloured area is a button. Looking at a button presses it: the part it names moves at once, hard, and then eases back a little. Each click presses it again. The brain's right half is in cool colours and moves your left side; its left half is in warm colours and moves your right side. One short line at the top gives you one small thing to try at a time.

- **Look** with the mouse or trackpad. Your head and eyes turn while your trunk stays put. Look down to see your chest and legs, or press E to look from outside. The muscles that turn your head light as you look. Your left hand keeps its place in the view.
- **Look** at a coloured motor area and rest there for a moment. The movement under your look happens at once: the part is driven hard to its end, rebounds, and eases back a little. **Click** to do it again; every click repeats it. The area and the muscles it drives light up together. After you try a movement, its area and muscles rest a shade darker, so you can see what is left to try. A cone of light in the same colour runs from that part of the area to each muscle it moves. Your look reaches an area even when your body or the mirror body is in front of it.
- **Try the left wrist.** Look at Left wrist on the brain’s right half. The tip beside the crosshair names the area and the movement under your look. Each area has a part for each of its movements: move your look a little within the area and the tip names another one.
- **Read what you are looking at.** A small tip beside the crosshair names the motor area under it and the one movement your look does there, for example “Left elbow: Bend”. On a body it names the muscle or bone under the crosshair. That tip, the short line at the top, the names on the brain and one small card are the text the game starts with. **R** opens the fuller cards, then hides them all, then shows the small card again.
- **Press and drag one of your limbs.** Press on an arm or a leg and drag: sideways turns it, up bends it and down straightens it, as far as you drag and through the joint’s whole range. (Keys 1 to 9 still do an area’s movements in their order for whoever likes them; the game no longer lists them.)
- **← and →** put your look on the next area toward that side of the screen. Hold a key to keep stepping. The walk stops at the end of the strip and waits while Space holds something. If a compass arrow crosses the selected area, the arrow fades. Click to use the compass arrow, or Shift-click to press the selected area behind it.
- **Bring a part back** by switching on the opposite movement, whose area glows faintly, or with **Reset body** on the Esc page. A part held one way first comes back to where it started; switching on the opposite once more takes it the other way.
- **Head and trunk.** A trunk movement bends the trunk under your head, and a head movement turns the head under your eyes: your view stays where your pointer put it, and the mirror body shows the head move. In this game a head turn is brought back from the neck area on the other half of the brain.
- **Legs.** A knee bends all the way, and the hip area swings the thigh down and back as well as up, out and in. A leg that swings down can leave your own view; watch it on the mirror body.
- **Click a muscle** on your own body or on the mirror body to switch on the motor area that moves it. The “Looking at” card names the muscle, bone, area or arrow under the crosshair before you click. Bones do nothing.
- **W/A/S/D** move your left hand: W forward, S back, A left, D right. The shoulder part of the left-arm strip lights (and the elbow part for W and S), with the muscles that make the movement, and the information card names the key, the strip part, the movement and the muscles.
- **Hold Space** on the brain, the mirror body or one of your limbs to take hold of it. Your left hand closes while you hold the object. The right hand closes when you press the mouse button. Then **move the pointer** without pressing a mouse button: your view turns and carries the held thing with it. The mirror body lifts off the ground this way. **W** moves the held thing farther, **S** nearer, and **A/D** sideways. The keys keep the held item at its current height. The hold keeps its thing wherever the crosshair goes. Let go of Space to leave it there.
- **Tap Space** once to take hold and keep holding after the tap. Tap Space again to let go.
- **The mirror body.** Take it by any part and you move all of it: Space carries it and a drag turns it.
- The game starts with the underside of your limbs showing: palms up, feet turned 45 degrees outward like a V. **Tap Space twice** and all four limbs switch quickly to the overside (palms down, feet turned 45 degrees inward like an A); tap twice again and they switch back. The foot leads the leg's turn, then the shin, then the thigh. The whole arm turns over, the hand first and the upper arm after it, so the front of the arm shows on the underside and its back and outer side on the overside. **Tap twice and hold** and the limbs keep turning slowly between the two sides. Let go and every limb stays where it is.
- **Hold Shift** as you click, press Space or drag to reach the other thing on the same line of sight: the muscle in front of a motor area, the brain behind the mirror body, or what is behind an arrow.
- **Press and drag** on the brain or the mirror body to turn it. Drag the mirror body sideways to turn it round, all the way if you like, and up or down to tilt it, until you see it from straight above or below. While Space holds either object, hold the mouse button and drag to turn it. W/A/S/D keep carrying it. On your own limbs, drag sideways to turn, up to bend and down to straighten. Where you press decides which joint bends. Up and down bring the left hand nearer or farther.
- **Click an arrow** for a small step, or hold it to keep going. The grey arrows turn the brain in place. The green arrows by the mirror body turn it round (left and right) or tilt it (the top one shows it more from above, the bottom one more from below). A turned or tilted mirror body stands a little farther back where it needs the room. Reset brain view on the Esc page stands it upright again, facing you. Press Esc twice for a free pointer if you want to click them while you watch.
- **E** switches to an outside view from behind your head, and back. The mirror body moves away by the same distance as the camera, so it does not end up close to the camera. Let go of what Space holds first: while Space holds something, E only shows a line that says so.
- **R** shows more information. The game starts with a short reading card and the tip. Press R for More detail and the colour key. Press R again to hide the cards, or once more to bring back the short card.
- **Esc** pauses and shows the instructions. **Reset body** puts every body part back where it started. **Reset brain view** puts the brain and the mirror body back. The settings there change the look speed, whether up and down are flipped, the picture quality and the lifted outer muscles, and hide or restart the “Try this” guide. Thin outlines between limbs are drawn at every picture quality; the soft shading in the creases between muscles is drawn only at full quality.

Brain and muscle signals are simulated and unitless. A clicked muscle lights a whole motor area, not a spot for that one muscle. A movement staying until its opposite is a rule of this game. Movement limits, physiological mappings and movement mechanics are simplified and still need checking.
