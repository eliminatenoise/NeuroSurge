# NeuroSurge player guide (simple)

[Play Full](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/scene/example/preview.html) · [Play Lite](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/scene/example/preview.html?edition=lite) · [Technical guide](PLAYER-GUIDE-TECHNICAL.md)

## Move a body part from the brain

1. Open either edition in a desktop browser with a keyboard and mouse or trackpad. Press and hold on an empty part of the picture, then drag to look around. Let go to stop turning.
2. Point at a coloured piece of the brain and let the pointer rest there. The coloured pieces show the **motor strip**, a region of the brain that helps control movement. A label beside the pointer names the part, and that part of the body moves. For example, a shoulder area moves the shoulder. Click on the area to repeat the move. Press **Left Arrow** or **Right Arrow** to aim at the next area in that direction. The mark in the middle of the picture then does the pointing until you move the mouse again.
3. Watch your body and the mirror body. Both make the same movement. The mirror body gives you another view of it. Press **E** to change between the eye view and the outside view. In the eye view, moving the mouse turns the camera from one fixed place. From outside, moving the mouse takes the camera around the body at a steady distance. The game leaves the view where you set it until you move it again.
4. Press **Escape** to open the controls. Open **View tutorial** to follow the steps. The tutorial can mark a visible motor area and the part of the body it controls. You can resume or restart the tutorial in the same menu.

The **Resume** button keeps the pointer free. To turn the view with every mouse movement, choose **Capture mouse** in the controls. Press **Escape** to release it and open the controls again. If capture is unavailable, drag on an empty part of the picture to keep playing.

If you turn far enough that the brain and both bodies leave the picture, a message says you have turned away. Drag the empty space back toward the brain, press **Left Arrow** or **Right Arrow** to aim at a motor area, or press Escape and choose Reset brain view. If you turned up or down, the opposite **Up Arrow** or **Down Arrow** also turns you back. While the brain is out of view, Reset brain view returns the camera to its starting direction. The tutorial and information cards receive your clicks, so you can read them without pressing a motor area behind them. When the tutorial shows a Brain ring, aim at it and press Space to pick up the brain.

A game link can still open when the app that shared it adds tracking information to the address. If the game shows an address error, select **Start the game** to open the usual address.

Drag on an empty part of the picture to turn your view. Finish the drag before aiming at a coloured area to start a movement. When the tutorial asks you to drag a limb, start at the centre of the ring and move the pointer while holding the button. If you hid the information cards, press R to show them again. If the ring stays hidden, turn the view until the limb is clear. If you turn away from the whole scene, the game shows how to look back, even with the information cards hidden.

You can turn the view with a finger in either edition. Touch an empty part of the game picture and slide your finger. Lift it to stop turning and leave the view where you put it. To start a movement, tap a coloured area of the brain and lift your finger without dragging. If the browser cancels the touch, the tap starts no movement. Keep a keyboard available for key commands.

## Move one limb yourself

During play, press **1** for the left arm, **2** for the left leg, **3** for the right leg or **4** for the right arm. Use **W**, **A**, **S** and **D** to move the selected limb. Release the key when it reaches the position you want.

From the view out of your body, look a little up or down to raise or lower the selected arm at the shoulder or the selected leg at the hip. You can do this without looking straight up or at the floor. After you select a limb, the aiming mark for this control sits about a quarter of the way down from the top of the screen. The mark stays there. The drawn wrist or ankle follows the joint, so it can move away from the mark when the limb reaches the end of its movement or the camera looks elsewhere.

Step 4 lets you practise this movement. Drag up or down on an empty part of the picture. If you chose **Capture mouse**, move the mouse up or down. With the keyboard alone, press **Up Arrow** or **Down Arrow**: each press turns your view a little up or down. If you clicked a long information card, these keys scroll the card instead; click the picture to use them for your view again. Watch the hand or foot on the limb you chose. The tutorial advances when the wrist or ankle moves at least 2.5 cm compared with its position beside the shoulder or hip just before you looked. The game gives it up to 0.35 seconds of game time to move. If you are looking from outside the body, press **E** to return to the eye view first.

Press and drag on a limb to move the joint you pressed on. Press **Space** to grip. While you grip, your left hand stays closed, so its finger and thumb areas do nothing until you let go, and the tip says so. Aim at the brain or mirror body and hold Space to carry it, then release to put it down. You can drag the brain or mirror body to turn it. Hold **Shift** on the initial press to reach the item behind the one you would usually select. With nothing behind it, Shift changes nothing.

## See what moved

Press **R** to show more information about the area you are looking at. Press it again to hide the cards, then once more to return to the short cards.

In the normal view, faint lines of light travel across the brain and through the motor strip. Press **M** for the detailed electrical map and again to return. In the Escape menu, untick **Electrical effects in the normal view** if you want a quieter picture. If you want to return the body or brain to its starting place, use the matching reset button in that menu.

The game draws its own example of brain and muscle activity. It has no measurement from a person. The body moves according to the rules built into the game, and the colours show those rules in action.

## Choose a download

Full and Lite have the same controls, body shapes and brain. Lite uses smaller files to deliver the same shapes. The five groups of anatomy files, their contents list and the body rig save 996,262 bytes, or about 1 MB. If your browser needs the 142,385-byte decoder to open them, the total saving is 853,877 bytes, or about 0.85 MB. For a local copy, follow the [launcher instructions](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/START.html).
