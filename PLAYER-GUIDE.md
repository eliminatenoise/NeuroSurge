# Play NeuroSurge

Open https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroSurge/. The page runs locally in your browser after downloading its files. See [preview notes](PREVIEW-NOTES.html) for what is new, the current limitations and credits. For offline local hosting, run `node serve-local.mjs` and open the printed address.

There is no start page. While the files load, which takes a few seconds, you watch the bones, the muscles and the brain come together, a bar with a percentage shows the progress, and the main controls stay on screen until the game is ready: W A S D move, Space grips, E changes the view, R shows information; on the trackpad or mouse, moving looks, a click presses and a drag turns. The game then begins by itself. Click the scene once so the pointer looks around; that first click does nothing else. Esc shows the instructions at any time.

You are slumped back as on a sofa, looking diagonally down your own front. From the bottom of the view up you see your collar bones and both shoulders, your chest and stomach, and your legs running out to your feet. The brain stands ahead of you and above your feet, large and beating slowly. You see it from above, turned to face you: its front is at the bottom and its right half is on your left. The coloured band across it is the motor strip, a simplified map of the body, shaped like a lowercase n: the leg areas at the top in the middle, the arms down each side, the face at the two lower ends. The mirror body sits facing you inside that arch, at your own size, its toes almost touching yours. Think of the strip as a control panel: each coloured area is a button. Looking at a button presses it: the part it names contracts at once, hard, and then goes back to where it was. Each click presses it again. The areas on your left are in cool colours and move your left side; the areas on your right are in warm colours and move your right side. The crossing is the usual one: the areas on your left are in the brain's right half, and each half of the brain mainly moves the opposite side of the body. Two eyeballs hang on their nerves at the brain's front to show which way it faces. At the start they are behind your own body: press E to see them from outside, or lift the brain with Space. One short line at the top gives you one small thing to try at a time.

- **M opens the activity map.** Play or pause one recorded right-wrist movement. Drag the time slider, or use its arrow keys for single samples, Home for the start and End for the last sample. Read the eight scalp sites on a common voltage scale. Choose a scalp or forearm contact to see its trace, or follow both encoder angles in the wrist plot. Open the forecast below the traces and choose one of six times. Compare the measured angles with predictions from wrist motion alone and with forearm or scalp signals added. **Game pathways** lets you choose a region and movement, see its motor and sensory routes, then try the simulated action. Esc returns to the game. In **Hand search**, open the wrist forecast to try an invented movement history or enter your own four earlier positions.
- **F opens a movement box.** Try “Bend my right elbow” and press Enter. You can bend or straighten either knee, wrist or elbow, open or close either hand, and spread the fingers on either side. The game uses its built-in movement map. Choose **Use voice** to dictate on a browser that supports on-device recognition, check what it heard, then choose **Move**. Esc or Cancel returns to the game. Type “Replay a forward jump” to see a laboratory recording. The same three recordings are available in **M → Whole-body motion**. To search the printer recording with your own words, type a description and choose **Find hand recording**. Select a ranked moment to inspect the tracked hand.
- **C lets your camera move an arm or knee.** Grant video access, then choose **Follow** in the small camera panel. You can also use the existing limb keys while the camera runs. Press **1** for your left arm, **2** for your left knee, **3** for your right knee or **4** for your right arm. The right arm is the first choice. For an arm, face the lens with your shoulders, elbow and wrist in sight. Raise your arm sideways or bend your elbow across the picture. For a knee, stand far enough back to see your shoulders, hips, knee and ankle. Turn partway sideways and first show a clear bend. Straighten and bend again. The preview looks like a mirror. Choose the side of your own body. Video stays on your device. Press **C** again or choose **Stop camera** to finish. [What the camera can follow](scene/src/camera/README.md)
- **Look** with the mouse or trackpad. Your head and eyes turn while your trunk stays put. Look down to see your chest and legs, or press E to look from outside. The muscles that turn your head light as you look. The hand or foot of the limb you chose keeps its place in the view: look up and the limb rises at the shoulder or hip, look down and it sinks, look to a side and it swings that way. Where its joints end the limb waits, and it joins your look again when you come back. In the view from outside (E) and while Space holds the brain or the mirror body, the look moves no limb.
- **Look** at a coloured motor area and rest there for a moment. The movement under your look happens at once: the part is driven hard to its end and rebounds off it, then goes back to where it was and stays there. **Click** to do it again; every click repeats it, also while the part is still on its way back. The area and the muscles it drives light up together while the part contracts and go dark as it goes back: nothing is lit for the way back. A movement that ends where the part already is, such as clenching a closed jaw, strikes its stop: a short twitch. After you try a movement, its area and muscles rest a shade darker, so you can see what is left to try. A cone of light in the same colour runs from that part of the area to each muscle it moves. Your look reaches an area even when your body or the mirror body is in front of it.
- **Try the left wrist.** Look at Left wrist, on the left side of the strip (the brain’s right half). The tip beside the crosshair names the area and the movement under your look. Each area has a part for each of its movements: move your look a little within the area and the tip names another one.
- **Read what you are looking at.** A small tip beside the crosshair names the motor area under it and the one movement your look does there, for example “Left elbow: Bend”. On a body it names the muscle or bone under the crosshair. That tip, the short line at the top, the names on the brain and one small card are the text the game starts with. **R** opens the fuller cards, then hides them all, then shows the small card again.
- **Choose a limb.** Tap or click a limb name at the bottom of the screen while the pointer is free. You can also press **1** for your left arm (the start), **2** for your left leg, **3** for your right leg or **4** for your right arm. The chosen limb itself glows for a moment on your body and on the mirror body. Your view turns only if the limb's hand or foot is outside the picture, and then just far enough to bring it in. **W/A/S/D** then move its hand or foot. A **double click** steps to the next limb, 1, 2, 3, 4 and back again; the second click presses nothing else. Keep the second click held to keep stepping slowly. A click or Space directly on one of your limbs chooses it too.
- **Press and drag one of your limbs.** Press on an arm or a leg and drag: sideways turns it, up bends it and down straightens it, as far as you drag and through the joint’s whole range. The limb glows while you drag it, and it becomes the chosen limb.
- **← and →** put your look on the next area toward that side of the screen. Hold a key to keep stepping. The walk stops at the end of the strip and waits while Space holds something. If a compass arrow crosses the selected area, the arrow fades. Click to use the compass arrow, or Shift-click to press the selected area behind it.
- **Nothing stays moved by the brain.** Every movement from the brain goes back by itself to where the part was before: where you put it with W/A/S/D, a drag or Space, or where it started. **Reset body** on the Esc page puts back what you placed.
- **Head and trunk.** A trunk movement bends the trunk under your head, and a head movement turns the head under your eyes: your view swings with it for a moment and comes back to where your pointer put it, and the mirror body shows the head move. Both go back by themselves.
- **Legs.** A knee bends all the way, and the hip area swings the thigh down and back as well as up, out and in. A leg that swings down can leave your own view; watch it on the mirror body.
- **Click a muscle** on your own body or on the mirror body to switch on the motor area that moves it. The “Looking at” card names the muscle, bone, area or arrow under the crosshair before you click. Bones do nothing.
- **W/A/S/D** move the hand or foot of the chosen limb. **W** reaches it out from the shoulder or hip and **S** draws it back in, along the line between the two as far as the joints allow (where they cannot keep the line, the limb still moves, more slowly and a little curved). **A** and **D** swing the whole limb to your left or right at the shoulder or hip without changing its bend. The limb does not turn inward or outward while you do this; the double tap of Space does that. At the start that is your left hand. Every limb goes through its whole reach whichever way you look: a hand out to a straight arm, back to your chest, across your body and out to the side; a foot out to a straight leg and back until the knee is folded. Your view stays where you put it; it turns only when the hand or foot would leave the picture. The part the keys move glows while you hold them, and the motor area for the joint that moves lights with its muscles. A hand stops at your own trunk. Your limbs pass through the brain and the mirror body. A limb that meets another of your limbs pushes it gently aside.
- **Hold Space** with the crosshair or free pointer on the brain or the mirror body to take hold of it. (Space on one of your own limbs chooses that limb.) While Space is down, both your hands close and the toes of both feet curl, whatever is under the crosshair, and each hand and foot glows as it closes. A click never closes a hand: it only presses. Your hands and toes also close a little as you look down and open as you look up. Then **move the pointer** without pressing a mouse button: your view turns and carries the held thing with it. The mirror body lifts off the ground this way. **W** moves the held thing farther, **S** nearer, and **A/D** sideways. The keys keep the held item at its current height. The hold keeps its thing wherever the crosshair goes. Let go of Space to leave it there.
- **Tap Space** once to take hold and keep holding after the tap. Tap Space again to let go.
- **The mirror body.** It starts as your reflection: your size, level with you, toe to toe. Take it by any part and you move all of it: Space carries it and a drag turns it.
- The game starts with the underside of your limbs showing: palms up, feet turned 45 degrees outward like a V. **Tap Space twice** and all four limbs switch quickly to the overside (palms down, feet turned 45 degrees inward like an A); tap twice again and they switch back. The foot leads the leg's turn, then the shin, then the thigh. The whole arm turns over, the hand first and the upper arm after it, so the front of the arm shows on the underside and its back and outer side on the overside. **Tap twice and hold** and the limbs keep turning slowly between the two sides. Let go and every limb stays where it is. As the underside comes up, the muscles that bend the limbs (the flexors) light for a moment; as the overside comes up, the muscles that straighten them (the extensors) do.
- **Hold Shift** as you click, press Space or drag to reach the other thing on the same line of sight: the muscle in front of a motor area, the brain behind the mirror body, or what is behind an arrow.
- **Turn clockwise or anticlockwise.** Press on the brain or the mirror body and drag round in a big circle: after most of a circle it starts turning the way you are circling, about the line from your eye, and keeps turning as long as you keep circling. Reset brain view puts it back.
- **Light-blue glass.** Whatever is hidden behind something shows through it as a light-blue outline: a hand under your body, the mirror body behind your leg, the motor strip behind your knee, a body behind the brain. The crosshair still takes what is in front, and Shift reaches behind; only a limb that is almost wholly hidden behind its own body, drawn as filled glass, is taken directly.
- **Press and drag** on the brain or the mirror body to turn it. Drag the mirror body sideways to turn it round, all the way if you like, and up or down to tilt it, until you see it from straight above or below. While Space holds either object, hold the mouse button and drag to turn it. W/A/S/D keep carrying it. On your own limbs, drag sideways to turn, up to bend and down to straighten. Where you press decides which joint bends: on the left upper arm, up and down raise and lower the arm at the shoulder; on the left forearm or hand they bring the hand nearer or farther.
- **Click an arrow** for a small step, or hold it to keep going. The grey arrows turn the brain in place: each brings round the side it stands on, so the upper one shows more of the brain's back and the lower one more of its front. The green arrows turn the mirror body round (left and right) or tilt it (the top one shows it more from above, the bottom one more from below). In a wide window, look to either side above its head for the green up arrow. Press **E** to see the arrows from behind. Green still turns the mirror body and grey still turns the brain. A turned or tilted mirror body stands a little farther back where it needs the room. Reset brain view on the Esc page stands it upright again, facing you. Press Esc twice for a free pointer if you want to click them while you watch.
- **E** switches to an outside view from behind your head, and back. The mirror body moves away by the same distance as the camera, so it does not end up close to the camera. If Space is holding the brain or mirror body, E lets go before changing the view.
- **R** shows more information. The game starts with a short reading card and the tip. Press R for More detail and the colour key. Press R again to hide the cards, or once more to bring back the short card.
- **Esc** pauses and opens the controls and settings. Press **Esc** again or activate **Resume** with the keyboard to keep the pointer free. Click **Resume** to capture the pointer. **Reset body** puts every body part back where it started. **Reset brain view** puts the brain and the mirror body back. The settings there change the look speed, whether up and down are flipped, the picture quality and the lifted outer muscles, and hide or restart the “Try this” guide. Thin outlines between limbs are drawn at every picture quality; the soft shading in the creases between muscles is drawn only at full quality.

The avatar’s coloured brain and muscle signals are simulated and unitless. A clicked muscle lights a whole motor area, not a spot for that one muscle. A movement that contracts and then goes back is a rule of this game. Movement limits, physiological mappings and movement mechanics are simplified and still need checking.
